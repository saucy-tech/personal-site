> While ye have light, believe in the light, that ye may be the children of light. — John 12:36 (KJV)

This is how Jesus ends His public ministry. Not a long sermon, not a theological treatise. A command, and then He hid himself. While you have light, believe in the light, so that you become children of light. That's the word He leaves them with before the cross.

## The Word "While"

"While" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Not when conditions are perfect. Not when everyone around you is doing the same thing. While you have it. Right now, in this moment, with the light that's available to you today.

The psalmist covers the same ground from a different angle: "The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?" That's not a description of a feeling. It's a declaration made in the face of something that should be frightening. David didn't write Psalm 27 from a comfortable place. He wrote it surrounded by enemies. The LORD is my light, therefore no fear. Not an emotion. A choice.

## Where the Light Goes

Walking in the light is easy when everyone around you is doing it too. The test is when darkness is the easier option. At work when honesty would cost you something. Online when the crowd is moving in a direction you know is wrong. In friendships where your faith creates distance and you feel the pull to just go quiet about it.

Jesus called his disciples children of light. That's a commission. Carry the light into the places it doesn't naturally go. Not because you have it all figured out, but because you know who the light is.

This week ends where it began: with a king who rode into Jerusalem knowing the cross was three days out. He came anyway. The light went all the way through the darkness and came out the other side.

Walk in it.

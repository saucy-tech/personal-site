> "He that heareth you heareth me; and he that despiseth you despiseth me; and he that despiseth me despiseth him that sent me." — Luke 10:16 (KJV)

Jesus didn't send the seventy out with a promise that everyone would be glad to see them. He sent them out with clear eyes about what was coming. Some towns would open their doors. Some would not. And He told them both things before they left.

That's worth sitting with. He could have sent them out with encouragement only — you'll see great things, people will respond, the harvest is ready. He said those things too. But He also said: some will reject you. And when they do, shake the dust from your feet and move to the next town.

## The Dust Is the Point

Shaking the dust wasn't a dramatic gesture of contempt. It was a practical one. Travelers who passed through hostile territory would shake the dust off their sandals at the border — a way of saying, I'm not carrying this place with me. Whatever happened here stays here.

Jesus gave the seventy a posture for rejection, not just a plan for success. He didn't say, keep trying. He didn't say, argue harder. He said, don't absorb it. Don't let someone else's refusal become something you drag into the next conversation, the next relationship, the next town.

The authority He gave them wasn't just for healing the sick and proclaiming the kingdom. It was also the authority to leave well. To recognize when a door is closed and not stand in the threshold. That's a harder kind of authority to walk in than the kind that does miracles.

## The Weight of What You Carry

Luke 10:16 raises the stakes in a way that's easy to rush past. When someone receives the message, they're receiving Jesus. When someone refuses it, they're refusing Jesus — and refusing the One who sent Him. The kingdom isn't a nice offer being gently extended. It's the most significant thing in the room. It arrives with weight.

That means the people who carry it carry real authority. Not the kind that demands attention or forces compliance, but the kind that can look at resistance, say this is not mine to carry, and keep moving toward those who are genuinely hungry.

## Where You're Tempted to Stay

The harder question Jesus is pressing on here isn't about the towns that reject you. It's about whether you keep moving to the ones that won't. It's easy to stay in the familiar, to circle back to the same relationships that have never responded, to keep trying to convince people who've already decided. Meanwhile, somewhere else, there's a harvest waiting.

Not everyone is at the same place in their openness to God. Some people are far from ready. Others are closer than they look. The question worth carrying into Tuesday is where you're spending your energy — and whether you're moving toward the hunger you can actually meet right now.

Where are you tempted to keep sitting with rejection instead of walking toward someone who's ready?

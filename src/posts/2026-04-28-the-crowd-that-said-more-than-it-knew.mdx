> Took branches of palm trees, and went forth to meet him, and cried, Hosanna: Blessed is the King of Israel that cometh in the name of the Lord. — John 12:13 (KJV)

## The City That Erupted

Passover had already packed Jerusalem. A hundred thousand pilgrims, maybe more, crowded inside the city walls. And word had gotten out: not just that a prophet was coming, but that a man named Jesus had called a dead man named Lazarus out of the tomb four days after burial. The Pharisees themselves said it out loud: "Look, the whole world is following Him."

So when Jesus rode in, the city erupted.

Palm branches meant something specific. In Jewish culture, they signaled national victory. When the Maccabees liberated Jerusalem from a foreign oppressor, they marched in with palm branches. Waving them at Jesus was a statement: here comes the one who will free us.

They were right. Just not about which kind of freedom.

## The Word That Held Everything

"Hosanna" is worth pausing over. We treat it as pure praise, but its roots are a cry: *Save us now.* It comes straight from Psalm 118, "Save now, I beseech thee, O LORD." The crowd was not offering calm liturgy. They were desperate, genuinely pleading to be rescued.

And they were. That same week. From something far worse than Rome.

Most of them never saw it. They wanted a general. They got a sacrifice. The same voices that shouted "Hosanna" on Sunday would be quiet, or worse, by Friday. Not because the faith in that moment was hollow, but because they were worshipping the version of Jesus they had imagined rather than the one who had arrived.

## Worship Him for Who He Actually Is

That tension belongs to us too. It is easy to come to Jesus with a list of what needs fixing rather than coming simply to worship the one who is already at work.

The crowd's instinct was right: come with your whole self, come with urgency, come with the honest cry of someone who needs saving. What Jesus asks is that we let Him be the King He actually is. Not the one we scripted. Not the one who solves only the problems we named.

Where are you holding back your worship? Where do you need to stop directing and start trusting? Come today like the crowd came into Jerusalem, with something in your hands that says you believe He is worth it.

He is.
